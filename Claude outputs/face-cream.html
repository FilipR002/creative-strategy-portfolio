Case study 01 · Slovenian DTC skincare brand · Meta Ads

Face cream for skin over 40

CPA 21 € → 15 € Conversion rate 3% → 5,5% Account ROAS 1,6 → 1,95

Context

A face cream for women over 40, sold direct in Slovenia and Slovakia, Meta only. Price around 20 €, with a 3-pack offer and over 700 reviews at 4,7.

When I took over, one before/after ad from the previous year carried most of the product's spend at a CPA of 21 €, frequency 3, and it was fading. Everything ever tested on the account was product-forward. Nothing had been tested in a structured way.

The objective: a new static set that lowers CPA and keeps ROAS above break-even, and a system that keeps producing winners after that.

What I did

Researched the account history, the competitors in Ad Library and the customer voice on forums. Decided which formats to keep, which to add and which persona to test first. Wrote the hooks, the primary texts and the headlines. Briefed the designer, one brief per concept. Built the campaign structure. Reviewed with the owner and the media buyer, and iterated through three rounds before production.

Source insight

  • AccountThe before/after format had done its job. Nobody had tested a skeptic angle, an identity angle or a category-critique angle.
  • Ad LibraryThe "needle versus cream" angle was saturated, one competitor alone had over 100 active ads on it. Creams with the same hero ingredient sat between 47 and 163 €, which gave us the price anchor.
  • ForumsWomen talking about creams they threw away, about not believing ads anymore, about every cream after 40 looking the same. Three pain points: skepticism, promise versus price, and skin that changes after 40.
  • Purchase data36 of 41 purchases were 45+. The persona was confirmed by the account, not assumed.

Concepts

Nine concepts across the funnel, each one visual with five text versions. Only the text changes between versions, so the hook is the isolated variable.

Top of funnel, three concepts:

  • GridThe identity angle."Skin does not need more. It needs different."
  • Hero + questionThe skeptic angle."How many creams have you already thrown away?"
  • TypographicThe category angle."You are not paying for the story. You are paying for the cream."

Middle of funnel: a "what they promise, what you get" comparison against the category, an ingredient callout, and a "who this is for" card.

Bottom of funnel: cart reminders written for five different reasons people abandon, the 3-pack, and real reviews quoted verbatim.

From brief to live

The briefs went to production. When the visuals came back I checked each one against its brief: does it carry the one thought, does the layout match the sketch, are the rules respected. What needed fixing went back with notes, the rest went live.

Results and learning

CPA 21 € → 15 €. Conversion rate 3% → 5,5%. Account ROAS 1,6 → 1,95.

The winners were concepts, not single ads. That is the point of the method: I judge the concept as a whole, not which of its hooks happened to get the credit.

After testing the angles and the formats, I had a clear picture of what worked: the question-led concepts and the typographic manifesto. Once I found that solid base, I built on it, so the product had something stable I could keep working on and test new ideas against.
