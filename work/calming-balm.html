Case study 02 · Equestrian DTC brand · UK, DE, NL

Calming balm for horses

8 personas 40 angles 120 hooks 3 test waves 7-format mid-funnel library 3 UGC scripts

Context

A scent-based calming balm for horses and riders, a roll-on applied over the nostrils before a stressful situation: loading, the farrier, clipping, a new place. Sold direct across the UK, Germany and the Netherlands, with the home market used for content.

When I came in, the account had run the same generic copy across five countries for eight months. The videos were the same footage re-cut into fifteen "concepts", retention was 3 to 9%, and the only two ads that ever worked were short organic reels. The category itself is skeptical by default: the loudest customers on the forums believe no calmer works at all.

The objective: a creative strategy from the ground up, built for three very different markets, that the brand could keep producing from after I handed it over.

What I did

Research across four foundations: the evidence, the competition per market, the customer voice, and the rules. Eight personas, forty angles with an opening line each, ten scripting frameworks, fourteen formats. Then three top-of-funnel test waves, a seven-format mid-funnel library, bottom-of-funnel visuals, three UGC scripts and a voice specification written from the founder's own revisions.

Source insight

  • EvidenceThe only real study in the category measured heart rate variability in eight dressage horses inhaling lavender. Small sample, real measurement, published. That earned one careful phrasing and closed the door on everything else. No studies exist for the other two ingredients, so nothing was claimed for them.
  • CompetitionEvery UK competitor sells powder or paste in the feed. Nobody sells a scent, one movement, one minute. Germany sells stress through physiology, so the German angle had to be measurable. The Netherlands had no competitor at all: an empty auction.
  • Customer voiceFrom the two largest equestrian forums. Six themes came out of it. The rider as half the problem was the only angle no competitor had.
  • RulesThe existing ads claimed fewer falls, fewer injuries and fewer vet visits. Those claims push a cosmetic product toward veterinary medicine under EU regulation, and they had to go. A forum thread also showed a ready buyer walking away because the ads used an AI-generated horse and the origin was hidden. Real horse, visible origin, became rule one.
"Calmers are a placebo effect on the owner."
"It's not a calmer, it just helps him deal with things better."
"I am an anxious rider and my gelding reads me like a book."

Positioning

Not "the best natural calming aid". Instead: it does not sedate, it buys the horse five seconds to think before it reacts. Three pillars under that: five seconds not sedation, pre-diluted and safe instead of mixing oils in the tack room, and it works on both ends of the lead rope. Origin as proof.

Concepts

Top of funnel, three waves of five statics, each wave one hypothesis:

  • Wave 1 · ProofRiders in this category buy from evidence, not atmosphere."Eight horses. One scent. Something changed in how they settled."
  • Wave 2 · SituationA recognisable moment stops the scroll better than proof does."He holds it together for forty minutes. Then he runs out."
  • Wave 3 · SafetyThe whole category never has to answer the safety question, this product does."Nothing goes into the horse."

One thought on the image, no product, no logo, no CTA. Real horse, natural light, nothing that looks like stock. UK and US text layers on every visual.

Middle of funnel, seven formats, three variants each, so the same argument can be tested in different shapes: us vs the category, features pointed out, problem to solution, text in context, benefit stack, listicle, education grid. Same off-white surface, same typography, real product on the same table in every one.

Bottom of funnel: price, offer and "introduced at home, used on the days that ask more". Real photography only, because the actual bottle cannot be generated.

UGC: three scripts, each a different format (customer story, research-led, demonstration), each with its own hook, so a failure tells you whether the problem is the format or the product.

Working with the founder

This project ran directly with the founder, not through an agency layer. I brought the research and the first two waves, she brought years of knowing that world from the inside: how her customers actually talk about their horses, what they will and will not believe, and where a line overstepped what anyone can promise about someone else's horse.

Her feedback gave me insights I could not have found on the internet. She corrected product facts, pointed out where a hook sounded like a supplement ad, and showed me the difference between a rider reading an ad and a rider trusting it. We went back and forth on the lines until we had versions we both stood behind.

That is how I prefer to work with a client: research first so the conversation starts from facts, then real back and forth, and a result that neither of us would have written alone.
